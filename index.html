Why we made it

We're a group of students in Oshkosh. Every spring, the same thing happens: you're walking to class or along the river, and a red-winged blackbird comes out of nowhere and dives at your head. Gulls do it too.

Everyone has a story about where it happened, but that knowledge only gets passed around by word of mouth. We wanted one place where people could share it, so you could find out where the birds are nesting before you walk past.

Wing Watch started as our software engineering group project. We built it as a real app because it's a problem we actually deal with, and we think it can help other people too.

They're not mad at you

They're parents. It's nesting season, and you walked too close to their babies.

Red-winged blackbirds

The males guard nests near water and tall grass. Get close and they'll dive at your head to scare you off.

Gulls

Gull parents dive at people near their chicks, and gulls that get fed by people learn that humans mean snacks.

How to stay unswooped

A few easy habits go a long way.

  1. Keep walking. Don't stop near the nest.
  2. Wear a hat or carry an umbrella.
  3. Don't feed the gulls.
  4. Check the map and go around the hotspots.

Please leave the birds alone. Don't touch or harm them or their nests. Wild birds like these are protected by law.
